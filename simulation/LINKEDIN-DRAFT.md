An 86% agreement score can hide a model that misses almost every problem.

I completed a simulation study in Roster Gap Lab: ten explicitly hypothetical staffing rules, 24 constructed schedules, and 336 day/shift decisions. A rule detector matched a separately implemented reference on every scored unit. Two local models then judged the same structured facts without seeing the answers.

Bolt reached 86.3% agreement but detected only 1 of 46 gap units—the same overall agreement as always answering “no gap.” Flint detected 32 of 46, but produced 219 false alarms and 71 invalid answers.

The useful result is the contrast between an easy headline metric and the errors behind it. I kept the frozen protocol, actual model responses, negative results, tests, and reproduction commands in the project.

This used synthetic schedules and fictional operational rules. It does not establish clinical validity or safe staffing. I used AI assistance for design, code, review, analysis, and writing; the original human-judgment study remains a separate project.

Code and paper: https://github.com/Ledbetter117/ed-roster-gap-lab
